The Assignment

Somebody has to break the curse

Every basketball season, a handful of arenas earn a reputation: blown fourth-quarter leads, ice-cold shooting nights, free-throw chokes in crunch time, buzzer-beaters that land on the wrong side of the scoreboard. Action Network built a 20-year study of late-October and Halloween-week games to find out which buildings actually back that reputation up in the box score, not just in fan folklore.

Now we need someone to do something about it. One winning applicant becomes Action Network's official Hardwood Hex Exorcist for opening week: full ritual gear, sage at the gates, lucky charms in the stands, and an original piece of curse-breaking content to show for it. This isn't about haunting anybody — it's about lifting the hex. Applicants are encouraged, though not required, to target a building our research flags as cursed; judges are scoring creativity and execution either way.

This is a skill-based promotional competition, not a sponsorship by or affiliation with any basketball league, team, or venue.

Need Inspiration?

Three ways to take on a cursed arena

The best entries will tie directly to the data. Here's what that could look like at three of the arenas our research flagged.

Toyota Center · Houston

The Cold Rim Advisory

Houston's field-goal percentage drops 4.5 points during this window, the steepest fall in the top 10. Play it as a deadpan weather report: a giant foam thermometer pointed at the rim, a hair dryer aimed at the hoop pregame to thaw the shooting, and a straight-faced “Cold Rim Advisory” delivered like breaking news.

American Airlines Center · Dallas

The Fourth-Quarter Séance

Dallas blows a 10-plus point fourth-quarter lead more often than any other arena in the top 10, at a 14.3% rate. Circle up in the parking lot with fellow fans around a tombstone reading “10-Point Lead, Rest In Peace,” and chant to keep the lead alive into the final buzzer.

Scotiabank Arena · Toronto

The Buzzer Ward

Toronto loses on a buzzer-beater more often than any other arena in the top 10, at a 12.5% rate. Build an oversized “:05” clock, tape a protective ward over the face, perform a mock exorcism on a stopwatch before tip-off, and hand out “anti-buzzer” wristbands to the section.

These are starting points, not requirements. Judges are scoring creativity and execution, not how closely you match the brief.

House Rules
